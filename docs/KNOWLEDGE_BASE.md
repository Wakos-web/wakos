6. HISTORY

This is where the school becomes particularly interesting.

1953 — Technical college

The school's own historical account says the institution started in 1953.

It says the establishment was the brainchild of colonial governor Andrew Cohen.

The original institution was a technical college.

Purpose:

To yield/provide skilled labour for the growing industries in Jinja.

This means the original identity of the institution was not primarily conventional secondary education.

It was established around technical skills and industrial manpower development. (MM College Wairaka)



7. 1958 — Technical function transferred

According to the school's historical account, in 1958 the technical status was transferred to Kyambogo.

The stated reason was poor enrolment.

The principal at the time is identified by the school as Mr. Lloyd. (MM College Wairaka)



8. Farm school period

After the technical-college phase, the institution became:

Muljibhai Madhvani Farm School

The school says it was headed by:

Mr. Denis John Wood

The farm-school phase is an important part of Wairaka's identity.

The institution was therefore involved in agricultural/practical education, rather than simply academic secondary education.

The school says graduates obtained certificates accredited to City Lloyds in the UK. (MM College Wairaka)



9. Why secondary education was introduced

According to the school's historical account, parents began wanting their children to obtain conventional secondary education.

The reasoning was that parents wanted their children to qualify for government employment, which generally required secondary education.

Parents therefore began demanding a secondary school.

The school says this was eventually granted in:

1970

The institution then became:

Muljibhai Madhvani College – Wairaka. (MM College Wairaka)



10. First head of the secondary school

There is an interesting detail in the school's history.

The former Farm School principal:

Denis John Wood

was asked to act as head of the new secondary school but declined.

The school says:

Mr. Mulwanyi Lameck

from Gulu Farm School was subsequently asked to take over as acting headmaster of the infant secondary school. (MM College Wairaka)



11. 1973 — Name becomes Wairaka College

In 1973, the Ugandan government abolished foreign names attached to institutions.

The Board of Governors therefore resolved to change the name.

The school became:

Wairaka College

instead of:

Muljibhai Madhvani College Wairaka. (MM College Wairaka)



12. 1982 — A-Level

In 1982, Wairaka College was upgraded to:

A-Level

This is important because the school has therefore had O-Level + A-Level status for more than four decades. (MM College Wairaka)



13. Madhvani name returns

The school says that following the return of Indians to Uganda after the change of government in 1986, the Madhvani family reclaimed the school's name.

It again became:

Muljibhai Madhvani College – Wairaka

which is the current name. (MM College Wairaka)



14. HISTORICAL DISCREPANCY

This is something I would not silently remove from the research.

There are conflicting accounts about exactly when the secondary school began.

The official school history says 1970.

But a former student, Deogratias Ojambo, wrote to New Vision disputing some historical accounts.

He said that he joined Senior One in 1973 and found Senior Two, Senior Three and Senior Four already operating.

Therefore, for the new website, I would either:

Option A: say "The secondary school was established in the early 1970s."

or

Option B: retain the school's official date of 1970.

Don't state both without explanation.
